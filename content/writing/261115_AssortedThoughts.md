Trying to get back into this whole blogging thing so will be doing a Sunday post sharing what I've been reading/looking at, and any thoughts I've had about them. 

I was at a friend's flat this afternoon hanging out and on the wall they had a poster that said 'May you live in interesting times'. A group of us agreed that we might want to live in slightly less interesting times, especially after a week like this one. 

The big discussion I've been keeping up with is the updating timelines associated with RSI. The Big Post was from [Dwarkesh](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/rsi) which basically outlined why he thinks superintelligence is nearing and that we are about to cross an important threshold. I'm not super aware of the technical innovations or process behind generating LLMs and how that whole process works. However, I have been getting increasingly concerned about the structure of society that comes after some form of AGI which leads to a meaningful decrease in demand for human labour. 

[Patrick](https://x.com/patrickc/status/2121771165912064379) and [Ruxandra](https://x.com/RuxandraTeslo/status/2121791860754804918) had a couple of good posts discussing this general issue - what happens to the motivation of people in a world without work as we know it, and what is the incentive structure to pursue excellence? This [paper](https://x.com/JulianDJacobs/status/2120939971414806766) by [Julian Jacobs](https://x.com/JulianDJacobs) is on the list for this week and appears to tackle parts of this question. There are a whole host of sub-questions associated with this that I'm becoming increasingly worried/concerned about. I finished Brave New World a few days ago and think that's contributing to the general dystopian vibe. Relevant q's are of the shape: 
- Writing, reading, thinking are essential components of engaging with the world - if we remove all incentive to do these are we not worsening the human experience? How do we ensure sufficient incentives remain for people to pursue these? 
- How do we stop everyone becoming addicted to short-form video? 
- How do we get people to choose friction in their lives? 

The underlying assumption behind a lot of these questions is that friction and challenge are intrinsically good for human wellbeing. And I think that's broadly true. MattY's [discussion](https://x.com/mattyglesias/status/2111833088389263591) of working from home from a few weeks ago feels quite relevant to this, particularly that while it might not feel better to be in an office, it probably makes us happiner is in the long-term. I've started the Economics of AI [course](https://www.coursera.org/learn/economics-of-ai) by Anton Korinek recently to help build my understanding and intuition of how AI may impact society at large. I think a lot of these types of questions will be resolved through the political process and that there's a lot of scope to move the needle on how the conversations and policy-making process goes over the coming years. 

The other big news was Starship's successful [mission](https://x.com/SpaceX/status/2121715216597827756). In some ways nicely juxtaposes with the first half of this post insofar as it might provide something for us all to do when the AIs takeover. This excellent [post](https://blog.caseyhandmer.com/2021/10/28/starship-is-still-not-understood/) by [Casey Handmer](https://x.com/CJHandmer) (which is actually 5 years old!) talks about some of the ways in which the space industry hasn't internalised what this actually means for space exploration. The cost to get into space is going to plummet, and therefore demand will increase substantially. I've seen bits floating around about the extremely high capacity SpaceX are building out their factories to be. Apparently the expectation is to be doing 1000s of flights per year at some point in the near future, opening up a huge range of projects which before would not have been feasible. I'm super excited about the general possibilities here but it's also been in the back of my mind how both technologies dominating the news are made in the USA, and lamenting the state of the UK/EU in response. 

There are some nice parallels between the UK/EU (more EU) response to American innovation and reactions to SpaceX's success from certain online commentators. First, an almost arrogant 'well, we didn't need that anyway', and then attempts to  downplay the achievement itself. I'm not a fan of Elon Musk by any stretch of the imagination, but it's almost impossible to play down what they've accomplished here - SpaceX has uniquely executed where no one else has and at massive scale. Broadly, I'm more upset about what it represents though - the US continues to want to grow (at least more than the UK and Europe), and is threatening to run away from the rest of us. It increasingly feels like it will be another American century, and the rest of the developed world is asleep at the wheel. I wish this wasn't the case.

On my radar for this week is catching up with [Alex Imas](https://x.com/alexolegimas)'s [blog](https://aleximas.substack.com/). His [post](https://aleximas.substack.com/p/are-we-approaching-an-intelligence) with [Luis Garicano](https://x.com/lugaricano) was bouncing around but I haven't found the time to read it yet and seems relevant to the RSI discussion. Similarly, I want to understand RSI better and will be seeking out some posts to help on that front - this [post](https://blog.cosmos-institute.org/p/recursive-self-improvement) by [Aashish Reddy](https://x.com/_AashishReddy) being top of that particular list. I also want to find time to start getting up to speed with the literature/writing on AI use in low and middle income countries. I've not done a huge amount on the development economics side thus far in my general reading and it would be good to know what's happening there. Finally, I've started Consider Phlebas by Iain Banks, my second read from the Culture Series. Hopefully it's less dystopian than the sci-fi I've had of late (Three Body Problem + Brave New World). 

Over and out.
